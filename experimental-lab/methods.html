Methods & nuisances

Target and meanings

The main estimate compares the mean outcome if treatment were set to 1 with the mean outcome if it were set to 0.

Let A be the binary treatment, Y the scalar outcome, and C the covariate vector in the graph's verified adjustment witness. Y(a) denotes the outcome under intervention A = a. The target is the average treatment effect (ATE) for the population represented by the observed cohort:

τ=E[Y(1)−Y(0)]

The graph supplies the causal assumptions that make this target identifiable from observational data. Identify checks those graph conditions without data. It does not verify that the supplied graph is true in the real world. Estimate approximates an identified quantity from observed rows.

A nuisance function is an intermediate function learned to compute the final effect. The three baseline choices use an outcome mean, an inverse-weight function, or both for each arm. The target uses the whole cohort, not just treated rows. No rows are trimmed to redefine the target.

Which method actually runs?

Read the result's actual method, rather than assuming every graph uses AIPW.

All three explicit baseline choices require one scalar binary treatment, one scalar continuous/binary outcome, fixed 0/1 regimes, no factual evidence and a verified baseline BD/AC witness. Every adjustment variable must be a treatment non-descendant. The actual witness is selected by construct_minimum_adjustment_set and checked by check_adjustment_criterion; the result records BD or AC and its adjustment variables. Only that witness's columns enter the nuisance functions. A vector witness node retains every ordered component.

A fit failure of the selected baseline method stays UNKNOWN. An explicit baseline choice on an ineligible graph/query returns UNSUPPORTED. There is no automatic fallback to the legacy estimator. The browser processes multiple outcomes separately on the same data snapshot. A Front-door or Sequential identification result does not itself enable any of these three baseline choices. The legacy compare, native run_demo and CLI demo retain their original plug-in/reference workflow.

Observed data and encoding

The estimators learn from the selected factual rows. Model references and benchmark truth never enter their fits.

load_data accepts the graph, selected numeric rows and an explicit mapping from each observed node to a column or ordered list of columns. A cluster binds a whole vector without changing the public node's name, dimension, arrows, positions or roles. A cluster must belong to an actually verified baseline adjustment witness and cannot be a treatment descendant. Unsupported mediator/time/action/outcome cluster bindings abstain.

Input limits are 1–5,000 rows, at most 100 selected physical columns and 500,000 cells. Duplicate/missing mappings, reused columns, missing values, nonnumeric/nonfinite selected cells and incompatible scalar/binary domains are rejected. Unused text columns can remain in the original file and are excluded before numerical loading. Rows are not silently dropped or imputed.

Nominal covariates retain their codebooks. An explicit categorical_encoding="one_hot" creates every category indicator in a temporary fitting view. It does not impose distances on ordinal-looking codes or turn indicators into new causal nodes. Original data, logical graph and codebooks remain intact. Dense temporary allocation is checked against 100 total columns and 500,000 cells. Categorical actions/outcomes are not supported for these mean contrasts.

For example, a three-level covariate coded 0, 1 and 2 is represented by all three indicator coordinates. estimation_details.inputs.covariates records the exact source-column/equality recipes. Each baseline choice uses only coordinates of its verified witness, not every variable drawn in the graph.

Feature basis

The baseline nuisance models use linear features and each feature's square, fitted with training-fold scaling.

For each outer training set, each supplied covariate coordinate is centered by its training mean and divided by its training standard deviation. A scale at most 1e-12 is replaced by 1. The standardized coordinates and their coordinatewise squares are concatenated; each resulting basis coordinate is again centered/scaled with training-fold statistics. The same transformation is applied to the heldout fold. Coordinates constant in training map to zero on the training fold; heldout rows still use that same transformation and need not be zero. There are no pairwise interactions, tree fit, neural network, automatic feature selection or truth-based tuning in these nuisance models.

Write this transformed basis as B(c). Every nuisance required by the selected method uses it, with separate arm-specific parameters. Outcome Regression fits no weights; IPW fits no outcome model. Outcome centering is additionally arm-specific. The basis retains every fitting coordinate of the witness, including every one-hot indicator; the twofold split does not discard features.

Outcome nuisance

For each treatment arm, a ridge regression predicts the mean outcome at the covariate values being evaluated.

The ideal outcome nuisance is m_a^*(c) = E[Y | A=a, C=c], the mean outcome conditional on the treatment arm and covariates. The fitted m_a(c) approximates that ideal; fixed-basis ridge fitting need not equal it. For arm a, use only its rows in the outer training fold. Center the basis by that arm's basis mean and the outcome by that arm's outcome mean. Fit slopes by least squares with a fixed ridge penalty of 0.001; the intercept is unpenalized. 

X = B_train_arm - mean(B_train_arm, axis=0)
y_centered = Y_train_arm - mean(Y_train_arm)
beta = solve(X.T @ X / n_arm + 0.001 * I,
             X.T @ y_centered / n_arm)
m_a(c) = mean(Y_train_arm) + (B(c) - mean(B_train_arm)) @ beta

This minimizes half the mean squared residual plus 0.001 / 2 * ||beta||². It is an unweighted arm-specific regression. Binary outcomes use the same least-squares nuisance, not logistic regression. Its predictions are not clipped. train_outcome_rmse is training fit error, not heldout validation accuracy or an uncertainty interval.

Weight nuisance

For each arm, calibration learns positive correction weights that approximately match the whole training cohort's selected moments.

The ideal inverse-weight nuisance is alpha_a^*(c) = 1 / P(A=a | C=c), the reciprocal of the conditional probability of receiving that arm. The fitted, constrained alpha_a(c) approximates this ideal; the two are not assumed equal. These are two separately learned inverse-weight functions, α0 and α1. The code does not separately fit a propensity probability e(c), and the two fitted functions need not be reciprocals of a single coherent probability model.

Let T denote the outer training rows, nT their count, and Iia equal 1 when row i has treatment a, otherwise 0. Let sa(c) = ba + B(c)Tθa. The fitted link is globally bounded:

αa(c)=1+19σ(ba+B(c)Tθa),1≤αa≤20

Here σ is the logistic sigmoid, implemented by stable expit. The cap 20 defines the fitted function class; weights are not clipped after fitting. For each arm the code minimizes this convex objective, with slope penalty fixed at 0.01: 

s_i = intercept + B_i @ slopes
loss = 19 / n_train * sum_train_arm(logaddexp(0, s_i))
       - 1 / n_train * sum_train_other_arm(s_i)
       + 0.01 / 2 * (slopes @ slopes)

The intercept has no penalty. With φ = (1, B), the gradient is the training mean of (I_arm * alpha_arm - 1) * phi, plus 0.01 * slopes on slope coordinates only. The intercept's first-order equation learns weighted arm mass nT. The slope equations balance selected moments against the full training cohort up to regularization and solver residuals. The actual mass residual is reported; no normalization is imposed afterward.

A finite intercept requires the training-arm prevalence πa to satisfy 1/20 < pi_a < 1. Slopes start at zero; the intercept starts at log((1/pi_a - 1) / (20 - 1/pi_a)). The numerical solver is SciPy’s limited-memory Broyden–Fletcher–Goldfarb–Shanno routine with box-constraint support (L-BFGS-B); no explicit coefficient bounds are passed here. It uses maxiter=400, ftol=1e-12, gtol=1e-7; the accepted final maximum absolute gradient must be at most 1e-5. The fixed cap, penalties and basis are not selected using model truth or benchmark references.

The bound prevents the former exponential-quadratic extension from exploding on a heldout outlier. It can also exclude true inverse weights above 20, creating weight-nuisance misspecification. Neither bounded weights nor good moment balance proves real-world overlap or causal correctness.

Cross-fitting and seeds

Each row's score uses functions fitted without that row.

A seeded permutation within each treatment arm allocates rows alternately to two outer folds. For each heldout fold, preprocessing, only the nuisance fits needed by the selected method use the other fold. AIPW fits two arm-specific outcome regressions and two calibration functions per fold; Outcome Regression fits only the regressions; IPW fits only calibration functions. The resulting functions are evaluated on the heldout rows. This is treatment-stratified twofold cross-fitting; there is no inner cross-validation or fitted hyperparameter selection.

The actual split seed is the dataset seed XOR 0x5A5A5A5A, recorded in estimation_details.options.seed. Changing the dataset seed can change generated rows and/or fold allocation; copying the retained CSV plus its recorded split seed reproduces the fit without rebuilding a model. A model seed fixes the generated mechanisms and is separate from the observed-data seed. Model-reference simulation uses a separately derived seed.

Three scores and their final averages

AIPW combines predicted outcomes with weighted observed prediction errors. Outcome Regression averages predictions; IPW averages weighted observed outcomes.

For each row i, evaluate the heldout nuisance functions at Ci. The 1-versus-0 row score is:

score_i = m_1(C_i) - m_0(C_i)
          + I(A_i == 1) * alpha_1(C_i) * (Y_i - m_1(C_i))
          - I(A_i == 0) * alpha_0(C_i) * (Y_i - m_0(C_i))
estimate = sum(score_i) / n

Outcome Regression (Plug-in)

score_i = m_1(C_i) - m_0(C_i)
estimate = sum(score_i) / n

This uses the same cross-fitted ridge outcome nuisance and witness coordinates as AIPW. It does not call the calibration solver or compute weight feasibility, ESS, weight mass, concentration or balancing diagnostics. It requires at least 128 rows and 32 training rows in each arm. Predictions can extrapolate; the absence of weight diagnostics is not evidence of overlap or correct outcome modeling.

Inverse probability weighting (IPW)

score_i = I(A_i == 1) * alpha_1(C_i) * Y_i
          - I(A_i == 0) * alpha_0(C_i) * Y_i
estimate = sum(score_i) / n

This uses the same bounded-calibration weight nuisance, cohort target and weight safeguards as AIPW. It does not solve an outcome regression or report outcome-fit RMSE. It is raw IPW: there is no division by an arm's sum of weights (Hájek normalization). The separately fitted inverse weights need not define a single coherent propensity probability model. Fixed basis, regularization and the fitted bound can bias IPW.

The denominator is the total number of observed rows, not the number in one arm or the sum of heldout weights. There is no heldout weight renormalization, score clipping, trimming or model-truth correction. AIPW separates outcome_contrast_mean from augmentation_mean; Outcome Regression reports only the former, and IPW reports raw_weighted_outcome_contrast_mean. A 0-versus-1 request reverses the sign. Identical requested levels return zero without nuisance fitting after the minimum-row check; this is the contrast of the same regime with itself.

In the browser, selected actions that are not ancestors of an individual outcome can be removed from that outcome's request without changing the graph or data. Under auto or legacy_knn API dispatch, if both resulting action maps are empty, the original observed-mean calculation returns the same-data zero contrast. An explicit aipw, outcome_regression or ipw request can instead be UNSUPPORTED when no applicable baseline action remains. This graph-dependent conclusion is separate from learning nuisance functions.

Diagnostics, warnings and failures

A failed fit has no estimate. A warning accompanies a finite approximation and explains its limits.

Baseline check Actual rule
Data and arm counts All three: at least 128 total rows and each outer training arm at least 32. AIPW/IPW additionally require at least 8 rows per heldout arm.
Finite calibration feasibility Each training-arm prevalence strictly greater than 1/20 and less than 1
Numerical fit Successful solver; finite objective/gradient/weights/predictions/score; final gradient maximum ≤ 1e-5
Heldout effective sample size (ESS) Fail below max(10, 0.05 × heldout arm count); warn below 0.25 × that count
Heldout concentration Fail if largest observed-arm weight share > 0.20; warn above 0.05
Training imbalance Fail if maximum absolute moment residual > 0.5; warn above 0.1
Heldout mass Warn if observed-arm weight sum / heldout fold size is outside [0.5, 2]
Cap saturation Report the fraction of observed-arm weights ≥ 0.99 × 20 in training and heldout arms; warn if heldout fraction > 0.05
Deadline Request budget is positive and ≤ 30 seconds; on exhaustion return no partial value

Calibration feasibility, solver, ESS, concentration, imbalance, weight mass and cap-saturation checks apply only to AIPW/IPW. Outcome Regression does not fit weights and does not run those checks. Its finite-input, arm-count, ridge-solve, score and deadline checks still apply. nuisance.used states which nuisance types the method requires. fitted becomes true only after all four required arm/fold fits of that type complete; identical regimes leave both false. fit_calls records actual attempts (including partial attempts on failure), while fit_completed counts completed fits. A complete fit has outcome/weight call counts 4/4 for AIPW, 4/0 for Outcome Regression and 0/4 for IPW. Identical regimes make 0/0 calls.

For positive observed-arm heldout weights w, ESS is sum(w)² / sum(w²) and maximum share is max(w) / sum(w). These describe concentration, not a new physical sample size or an accuracy guarantee. Mass uses the full heldout-fold denominator; the arm's raw inverse weights should represent the whole cohort. Logit ranges and training mass residuals help diagnose the fitted link.

Thresholds are engineering safeguards, not a positivity test or a statistical confidence guarantee. The code supplies no standard error or confidence interval for the observed-data AIPW, Outcome Regression, IPW, kNN or UCA estimate. Approximation, misspecification, fixed regularization and finite samples can leave bias even when status is OK. Failed supported fits return UNKNOWN; unsupported query/compiler/domain branches return UNSUPPORTED. Invalid input or stale IDs raise a validation error.

Legacy adjustment kNN g-computation

This branch predicts an outcome mean from nearby observed training rows, then averages those predictions over the other fold.

_estimate_mean first obtains and validates an adjustment witness. It uses a seeded twofold permutation of all rows, not treatment-stratified folds. Binary/categorical interventions and finite adjustment strata are matched exactly. Continuous interventions and continuous adjustment coordinates form the distance features. For each heldout stratum, the eligible opposite-fold source rows determine: 

k = max(8, floor(sqrt(n_eligible_training_rows)))
required: n_eligible_training_rows >= 2 * k

Features are standardized by the eligible training rows' means and standard deviations; zero standard deviations become 1. Squared Euclidean distances choose k neighbors by argpartition. The conditional prediction is their uniform mean, not a Gaussian kernel or learned outcome ridge. If there are no varying continuous features, the eligible arm/stratum mean is used. Constant features that cannot cover the requested values fail.

Each heldout row keeps its own adjustment covariates while continuous action coordinates are replaced by the requested intervention. Requested continuous actions must be inside observed global and training-stratum action ranges. Too few exact-match rows, nonfinite scales/distances/values or deadline exhaustion yield no estimate. A warning appears if more than 5% of rows need a neighbor radius greater than three times their factual-query radius (squared-radius ratio > 9). This cannot diagnose every form of bias.

The branch averages heldout predictions over all rows. With no action, it uses the observed mean directly. A mean difference computes the two interventional means with the same split seed and subtracts them; the first receives half the initially remaining time budget. For a supported event probability, target-row comparison indicators replace outcomes and joint-event hits are averaged. Events combine selected coordinate comparisons with logical AND. Continuous targets allow inequalities; == and != require a binary/categorical observed-data target.

No inverse-probability nuisance, balancing fit, residual augmentation or data standard error is present in this branch. Read each arm's actual method: when a conventional adjustment witness cannot be constructed, the code tries the general-ID compiler described next.

General-ID normalized-kernel plug-in

This is a separate, approximate integration procedure for supported identified formulas. It is not baseline AIPW.

_estimate_uca_mean calls the graph analyzer with up to 2 seconds of the remaining budget, max_orders=256 and max_mc_unions=256. The query must be marked identifiable. Tian, WMC and MC are the implementation’s identification-producer labels; MC here is not a Monte Carlo sampler. It tries accepted complete producers in the order Tian, WMC, MC; MC also requires a complete indexed formula. Identification alone is insufficient: the estimator's compiler must support the particular formula.

_uca_schedule freshens summed slots and requires a directed acyclic schedule of normalized observational kernels. It accepts sums, products, observational probability kernels and the number 1. It rejects ratios, unexpanded Q kernels, extraction operators, addition, unresolved/cyclic slots or duplicate suppliers. Limits are 2,000 visited expression nodes, depth 96 and 64 kernels. A formula may be available for display but unavailable for this numerical compiler.

There are two seeded, unstratified folds. Conditional kernels use exact discrete context matching in the opposite training fold, with the same k=max(8,floor(sqrt(n_source))) and n_source≥2k rule. Continuous context features use source-group means/standard deviations; values with standard deviation ≤ 1e-12 are treated as constant and must match the query. Neighborhood weights are uniform and normalized to sum to one.

Unconditional kernels use the heldout evaluation fold's empirical marginal. They do not fit a conditional regression on the opposite fold. For a terminal mean stage, the code uses the source/neighbor mean. If a generated head is used as a later context, or the request is an event probability, it draws an entire source/neighbor row uniformly, preserving coordinates of that head. Later kernels consume those generated values. The final targets or joint-event indicators are averaged across both folds, using the observed number of integration rows rather than the model-reference draw count.

For continuous contexts, the code records off-range queries and neighbor-radius coverage. A fixed intervention outside its training context range is allowed by default via allow_continuous_extrapolation=True, flagged as extrapolated. Setting it false withholds the approximation. Constant-context mismatch still fails. Coverage warns if any kernel/fold has more than 5% off-range queries or a radius above three times its median matched-factual radius. Nonfinite values, magnitude above 1e150 in checked matrices, sparse exact contexts and time/compilation limits withhold results.

There is no fitted propensity model, inverse-weight correction, balancing loss, AIPW augmentation or observed-data uncertainty estimate here. Bias and integration/smoothing error remain possible.

Generated-model reference and Monte Carlo error

A Random Data reference evaluates the fixed simulated Structural Causal Model (SCM), a collection of generated mechanisms and noise laws. It is not learned from the observed sample.

sample_reference is a separate action requiring a current Random Data model token, sample ID, public graph and any active random-binding key. It is unavailable for uploaded CSVs. It uses the same fixed mechanisms without replacing or modifying stored observed rows. Its seed is the observed data seed XOR 0xA5A5A5A5, distinct from the nuisance split seed.

Verified scalar linear expectation

_reference can propagate analytic means through eligible scalar linear mechanisms without factual evidence. It checks the actual generated function identity, coefficients, parent set and supported Normal/Uniform noise mean. If every required ancestor passes, approximate=False and reference_kind="exact_model_mean" identify an analytic model answer. This is not a claim that the real-world causal effect is known.

Explicit-vector paired simulation

The bounded Random baseline-vector adapter uses the existing sample_interventional method for two action worlds of the same model with the same per-variable exogenous random streams. It averages the rowwise outcome difference. Its Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE) is the sample standard deviation of those paired differences, with ddof=1, divided by the square root of the reference draws. approximate=True and reference_kind="monte_carlo_model_mean" distinguish this from an exact answer.

Reference draws are 256–10,000 and separately requested from the observed Records count. Defaults are 512 when a random cluster exceeds dimension 8; otherwise at least 2,048 or the observed row count, subject to request validation. Both vector reference frames together are limited to 100 physical columns and 500,000 cells. Time/nonfinite/shape failures expose no partial reference value.

Advanced SCM query path

Outside the verified analytic branch, the original SCM.query uses generated-model Monte Carlo. With factual equality evidence it uses sequential residual likelihood weighting and shared binary innovation thresholds; it does not learn nuisance regressions from the CSV. It normalizes particle evidence weights, requires ESS ≥ 100 and maximum particle share ≤ 0.1, and can reject unsupported custom mechanisms/densities or unresolved event thresholds. Those particle weights are different from AIPW's learned correction weights.

The original SCM path reports simulation standard errors from its actual particle calculations. The paired-vector path reports MCSE for its paired draws. Neither is a confidence interval or standard error for the observed-data effect estimate. A larger simulation draw count reduces simulation error; it does not repair confounding, nuisance bias or sparse observed support.

Actual source and exact-data reproduction

Use the current retained CSV and the code copied from that result's Details.

Details records the actual method, source SHA256, callable, requested method, split seed, contrast, current source-column/one-hot recipes and fixed options. The source-copy action verifies the fetched implementation's SHA against the run. The standalone script contains the actual implementation and constructs the same numerical inputs from the retained factual CSV. It calls estimate_balancing_aipw(..., estimator=options.estimator) directly; the recorded value is aipw, outcome_regression or ipw, so replay selects the same score and nuisances; it does not regenerate data or consult a reference. Floating-point optimizer results can vary slightly across numerical runtimes.

For a legacy result, replay uses the actual graph, mapping, encoding, data seed, query and estimation_method with Editor.request("load_data", …)/Editor.request("estimate_data", …). Its implementation source is the original graph estimator, not the bounded AIPW module. The native wheel bundles the same source and this document. A pasted notebook/exec context can compute without a source file; its unverifiable source fingerprint is explicitly null.

  • Actual three-method baseline implementation: estimate_balancing_aipw
  • Data adapter and legacy methods: _balancing_baseline, _estimate_mean, _estimate_uca_mean, _uca_schedule, _reference, _paired_vector_reference

  • Graph-only identification and formula metadata

CSV replacements, edited graph/types, changed column bindings, new models or new generated samples invalidate the associated results/IDs. Head and Download CSV reuse the stored data snapshot. A model reference never replaces a failed observed-data estimate.

Source grounding and limits of guarantees

These primary research sources explain related calibration, augmented-score and cross-fitting ideas:

  • Hainmueller (2012), Entropy Balancing for Causal Effects
  • Tan (2020), Regularized calibrated estimation of propensity scores with model misspecification and high-dimensional data
  • Chernozhukov et al. (2018), Double/debiased machine learning for treatment and structural parameters

The bounded-logistic link, fixed L2 penalties, chosen basis and engineering gates here are the actual implementation choices. This code is not presented as the exact algorithm or theorem implementation from the entropy-balancing or calibrated-propensity papers. Double-robustness and double/debiased-machine-learning inference require conditions on causal assumptions, overlap, nuisance approximation/convergence and empirical processes; this tool does not certify them from the dataset.

In particular, a fixed basis or weight cap can misspecify the nuisance functions, fixed regularization can leave bias, and a successful solver does not establish a causal model. There is no unconditional finite-sample unbiasedness, root-n inference, universal accuracy improvement or confidence-interval guarantee. Read the returned diagnostics, assumptions and actual computation for each query.
