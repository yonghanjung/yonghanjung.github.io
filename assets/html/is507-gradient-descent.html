Gradient descent on a real two-layer network

Network: h(x) = 1.5 · ReLU(2 · ReLU(ω x + b) − 0.5). Layer 1 has weight ω and bias b; layer 2 and the output are fixed, so the loss depends on two numbers and can be drawn.

Data: 60 simulated points from the same network with ω = 1.2, b = 0.3, plus noise. Loss: L(ω, b) = mean of (y − h(x))². Drag the hill to turn it; click the map to choose a start, then press Run.

Things to try: (1) start at ω = 0.5, b = −1.4: the first unit is always off there, the slope is exactly 0, and the walk never moves. (2) start at ω = −2, b = −1: the walk slides into that flat area and stops at a bad loss. (3) keep the default start and raise η to about 0.6 or more: the walk overshoots. Different starts can end in different places. (4) press 10 random starts: some walks get stuck in the flat area, and we keep the best one.
